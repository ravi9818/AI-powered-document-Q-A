Embeddings and Vector Databases - Notes

Embeddings are numerical representations of text, images, or data. They help computers understand similarity and meaning between words or sentences.

For example, the words "king" and "queen" have similar embeddings, while "king" and "apple" are very different.

Vector databases store these embeddings as vectors. When we search for something, the database finds the most similar vectors instead of exact keyword matches.

RAG, or Retrieval-Augmented Generation, combines these ideas. It retrieves relevant text chunks using embeddings from a vector database, and then an LLM generates an answer based on that retrieved information.

Steps in RAG:
1. Indexing: Convert documents into embeddings and store them.
2. Retrieval: Search for the most similar documents using embeddings.
3. Generation: Use the retrieved context to answer the question.

Popular vector databases include Chroma, FAISS, and Pinecone.

This technique helps large language models answer questions about data they were not trained on directly.
